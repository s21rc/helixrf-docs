    Build and first power-up¶

    How to wire the controller into your amplifier and check it before the PA supply is ever switched on.

    RF safety

    High RF power. Never operate without a proper load or antenna connected.

    What you need¶

    • A 12 V DC supply for the controller (see J22).
    • JST PH (2.0 mm) housings and crimp contacts, or wire for the solder pads.
    • Shielded cable or twisted pairs for the SWR bridge signals.
    • A multimeter.
    • A dummy load rated for the full output power, and a reference wattmeter for the power calibration.
    • A small screwdriver for the protection trimmers (RV1–RV5).

    Wiring¶

    Wire the modules to the controller as shown in Connectors and wiring. It has the system diagrams, and a pin-out and wiring sketch for every connector.

    Suggested order:

    1. 12 V supply (J22) and the display (J18).
    2. High Side Switch: sense (J8) and control (J10).
    3. LPF module: power (J39) and band select (J43 or J41).
    4. SWR bridges (J3, J5), NTCs (J28) and fan (J20).
    5. PTT and RX/TX relay (J26), PA bias (J30).
    6. Radio band data (J1 or J16), ALC and TX inhibit (J24), and the optional antenna selector (J45) and rotator (J32).

    First power-up¶

    Do this with the PA supply switched off and no drive from the radio.

    1. Before connecting the board, measure the 12 V supply: check its polarity and that it reads 12–14 V.
    2. Connect the 12 V supply and the display only. The power LED on the controller board lights, and the display shows the HelixRF splash screen, then the main screen. Measure the supply rails at the test points.
    3. Swipe down to open the menu and tap Info. Controller Firmware shows a version number when the display and the controller are talking. If it stays at 0, check the display cable (J18): the controller's TX must go to the display's RX. See Display and controller not talking.
    4. Connect the rest of the wiring (power off while you plug in).
    5. Power up again and open Diagnose. Check that both NTCs give a plausible reading and that nothing reads full scale (4095) with no signal applied. See Diagnostics.
    6. On the main screen, press and hold PA ENABLE until it turns bright green. (The PA supply is still off.)
    7. Key the radio with its power at zero. TRANSMIT lights at the top right and you hear the RX/TX relay change over. Unkey: the relay drops. With PA ENABLE off the relay stays in receive: that is the standby mode.

    Test the relay before connecting the drive

    Check that the relay changes over on PTT with no RF applied, before you connect the drive cable.

    Test points¶

    Seven test pads on the top of the controller board give quick access to every supply rail. Measure them with a multimeter against GND (for example J22 pin 1). The silkscreen label next to each pad is shown in brackets.

    Test point Rail Expected Supplies
    TP5 (R12v) +12 V input, after fuse F1 Your supply voltage Everything below
    TP6 (L12v) +12 V, filtered Your supply voltage LPF and antenna relay drivers, RX/TX relay
    TP4 (F12v) +12 V fan, filtered Your supply voltage Fan connector J20
    TP2 (6v6) +6.6 V 6.6 V Buck converter output, feeds the 5 V regulator
    TP1 (5v) +5 V 5.0 V Display, CAT, band-data and Neural supplies
    TP3 (3v3) +3.3 V 3.3 V Processor, comparators, logic
    TP7 (12v) −12 V Negative, close to your supply voltage ALC switch, pinch-off supply (J30 pin 6)

    If TP5 reads 0 V, check the supply polarity: the input fuse trips when the supply is reversed. If TP5 is fine but a lower rail is missing, the stage that makes it, or something loading it, is at fault. Disconnect the connectors one at a time to find the load.

    Then work through the setup in the order given in Getting started.
